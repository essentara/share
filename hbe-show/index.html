Hot Buttered Epiphanies · Show one-pager

What this show is

An advice column with IFS meditations & music at the end.

The format

Listeners send in letters. Lissa and I respond through an IFS lens. I create a musical mantra for each episode.

When the episode ends

The listener will have experienced an IFS meditation and listened to a mantra.

Listeners will be left with more spaciousness and self-compassion. They'll feel met and accompanied by us. We leave them a bit better than when they started the episode.

What it sounds like

Feel
More raw than produced.
Two people
The hosts are thoughtful, funny, and caring. They discuss themes with confidence and sensitivity. They pull out useful insights and perspectives for listeners. They take the work seriously and don't take themselves seriously.
Length
60 minutes including the meditation and mantra ideally. We record about seventy-five minutes and cut to sixty, max.
The ending
The end of the advice column portion is a gentle ramp-off from conversation to meditation & music.
Ads & CTAs
Calls to action and disclosures need to happen at intro and halfway through the episode.

The spine

Cold open → music sting → host intros → the letter → discussion → IFS-guided practice → original mantra

The faster we get into the letter the better.

What never happens on this show

We're never claiming that we know everything or have total authority. We are never claiming to have it all figured out.
We are never telling people what to do. We are sharing our perspective, but we are never prescriptive.
We are not salesy or promotional. Whenever we do promote is done with taste. It feels appropriate and relevant.
We are speaking truth to power, but we're never calling out. We're always calling in. We're never talking shit. We're not gossiping.
We're not undermining our own authority, our own perspective and experience.
We're never taking ourselves too seriously. We're never afraid to use humor. We're never using humor to hurt people. At least not intentionally. If our humor does hurt people and we hear about it, then we're never afraid to call ourselves in and apologize.
We never stop ourselves from speaking the truth because it might make somebody uncomfortable.

Good enough to ship

An episode ships when it has a good mix of humor and insight, and a beautiful meditation and mantra that leave listeners better than when they started.

An episode gets pulled when the core themes are buried under unrelated tangents and stories, and we're not actually answering the question. Distracted, hard to follow, bloviatory, long.

We can say "I don't know how to answer this" and talk about what you do when you're met with uncertainty. That's different from missing the point. That's different from not answering the letter.

The mantras

Each mantra is written for the person who wrote the letter. We are all connected and we all share these experiences, so we write for one person with faith that it will help somebody else.

A mantra accompanies the feeling the person is in. It makes a musical place to feel held and understood, however heavy or light that feeling is. It can hold the weight of a challenging feeling, or celebrate a feeling they want to move towards.

This is not a gimmick. I'm good at this. I know how to make music that makes people feel without being cheesy.

Who it's for

For

Seekers. People who care about emotion, connection, intimacy, power, and healing.

People who want to see themselves and others more clearly. People who know life is constantly changing, and that we are beautiful, brief, and imperfect.

Not for

People who want to stay surface level. People who want to be extractive. People who get off on the world being all about them.

People who are not ready to be challenged. People who don't want to look in the mirror and grow. People who are satisfied with the status quo of the world.
