Document two · The show brief

What Hot Buttered Epiphanies is.

An advice-column podcast with IFS practice and original music at the end. Thoughtful, funny, caring, and more raw than produced.

The format

A letter becomes a conversation, then a place to land.

Listeners send in letters. Dr. Lissa Rankin and Axel Mansoor respond through an Internal Family Systems lens. The conversation leads into an IFS-guided practice and an original musical mantra written for the person who wrote in.

Cold open → music sting → host intros → the letter → discussion → IFS-guided practice → original mantra
Sound

Real conversation, lightly shaped.

We record about 90 minutes and target 60 to 75. The edit should clarify the conversation without sanding off its life. The faster we reach the letter, the better.

The landing

Met, accompanied, more spacious.

The listener should leave with useful perspective, more self-compassion, and the feeling that somebody stayed with the question. No tidy bow required.

Editorial principles

The guardrails.

Invite. Do not prescribe.

We offer perspective without pretending to have somebody else's answer.

Take the work seriously.

We do not take ourselves too seriously. Humor connects. It never punches down.

Call people in.

We can speak directly about power without gossiping, posturing, or turning people into villains.

Keep the truth intact.

We do not flatten a difficult answer to make it comfortable or undermine our own experience.

Good enough to ship

The question gets answered.

An episode ships when humor and insight both have room, the central question stays visible, and the meditation and mantra create a beautiful landing.

It goes back into the edit when unrelated tangents bury the letter or the conversation becomes distracted, repetitive, hard to follow, or long for the sake of being long.

The audience

People willing to look.

Seekers. People who care about emotion, connection, intimacy, power, and healing. People who want to see themselves and others more clearly.

The mantras

Written for one person.

Each mantra accompanies the feeling inside the letter. It creates a musical place to feel held, understood, or moved toward something new. This is not a gimmick. Axel is good at this.
